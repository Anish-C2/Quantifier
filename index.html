Hall 02 · The atom of quantum information

A qubit is a direction on a sphere

A classical bit is a coin on the table: heads or tails. A qubit is the same coin still in the air.

|psi> = cos(theta/2)|0> + e^{i phi} sin(theta/2)|1>

theta tips the arrow from |0> (north) toward |1> (south). phi twists it around the equator.

Hall 03 · Many amplitudes, one shot

Hadamard builds a fair superposition

H turns |0> into (|0>+|1>)/sqrt(2). Both answers exist as amplitudes. A measurement then uses the Born rule: P = |amplitude|^2.

H|0> = (|0> + |1>)/sqrt(2) -> P(0)=P(1)=1/2

Hall 04 · reversible verbs

Gates rotate the state — they do not copy it

Every quantum gate is unitary: reversible and probability-preserving. Z only flips the sign of |1> — a phase you cannot see until it interferes.

H = Hadamard · X flips |0> and |1> · Z multiplies |1> by -1 · S is 90 deg phase · T is 45 deg phase.
Why this matters

Classical NAND throws information away. A quantum circuit cannot. That is why oracles in Grover and Deutsch are reversible phase tricks.

Hall 05 · two qubits, one story

Build a Bell pair, then measure both

Start in |00>. Hadamard the first qubit, then CNOT onto the second. The state becomes (|00>+|11>)/sqrt(2).

H on A, then CNOT A->B = (|00> + |11>)/sqrt(2)
What to notice

After a Bell pair you almost never see 01 or 10. The disagreeing states have amplitude zero.

Hall 06 · amplitudes can cancel

Two paths, one phase knob

Split |0> with H, paint a phase on the |1> path, then H again. Phase 0 returns |0>. Phase pi returns |1>. Grover is this idea at huge scale.

Hall 07 · one question, two answers

Deutsch: is the oracle constant or balanced?

Classically you must ask f twice. Deutsch asks once by sending both inputs through the oracle as a superposition and reading an interference bit.
